About

Most robots adapt to new tasks through software while their bodies stay fixed, but recent advances in morphing mechanisms, smart materials, and modular robotics make it possible for robots to change their shape, stiffness, and structure. This workshop focuses on reconfigurable intelligence, which combines adaptable robot bodies with the computational methods needed to design, select, assemble, plan, and control them. It brings together researchers in morphing mechanisms and materials, modular and collective robotics, embodied interaction, and learning and control, communities that have so far developed largely in parallel.

The workshop is organized around four topics: (1) morphing mechanisms and material intelligence, covering compliant, origami- and kirigami-inspired structures, smart materials, tunable stiffness, and metamaterials; (2) modular embodiments and robotic assembly, covering module design, morphology selection, assembly planning, and collective reconfiguration; (3) embodied interaction, sensing, and dexterity, covering proprioception, contact-rich manipulation, and multimodal locomotion; and (4) embodiment-aware computational intelligence, covering co-design, morphology planning, learning and foundation models across embodiments, and adaptive control.

This half-day workshop has two parts, Hardware and Software for reconfigurable intelligence, each with three invited talks and three late-breaking lightning talks, plus a poster and demo session and a closing panel of all six speakers on bridging the digital and physical domains. The workshop will produce a research roadmap, a list of open challenges and benchmark tasks, and a joint perspective paper, with the goal of building a lasting community around intelligent systems that can adapt their bodies as well as their behavior.
